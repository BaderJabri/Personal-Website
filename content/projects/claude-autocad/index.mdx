Drafting a part in AutoCAD is a long run of small, precise commands: a polyline here, an offset there,
a rectangle snapped to a construction line, then dimensions on their own layer. Claude × AutoCAD
exposes those commands as tools that Claude Code can call, so the conversation becomes the drawing.

## What the replay shows

The session on the home page drafts a station-3 wing rib for an SAE Aero plane. The airfoil is a
NACA 2412 at a 180 mm chord, sampled at 48 points. Spar slots sit on the camber line at 25% and 65%
of the chord, and each lightening hole is half the local thickness of the section so the rib keeps
its strength where it is thinnest.

Each step goes to AutoCAD as a tool call, and AutoCAD echoes it on its own command line: `PLINE` for
the outline, `RECTANG` for the slots, `CIRCLE` for the holes, then `DIMLINEAR` and `DIMDIAMETER`.
Every layer follows AutoCAD colour conventions: white outline, cyan cutouts, red centerlines and
yellow dimensions.
